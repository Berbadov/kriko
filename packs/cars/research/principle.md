Kriko surfaces the risks a used-car buyer **cannot cheaply get from a standard
pre-purchase inspection** (ekspertiz) — what to worry about for *this* car,
before they even book the expert.

Keep a claim when it is:

- **Specific to this configuration** — this engine code, this gearbox type, this
  fuel, this market. Not advice that applies to any car.
- **Predictable from the listing alone** — from mileage, age, fuel or gearbox,
  without inspecting the car. A known weak point the odometer implies.
- **Due unless the ad proves otherwise** — a cam belt, a major service, a clutch
  at high mileage. If the listing offers no evidence the work was done, that
  silence is itself the signal.
- **Expensive or dangerous** — structural failures and costly systems: emissions
  hardware, dual-clutch mechatronics, turbos, timing components.

Drop a claim when it is:

- A generic dashboard-warning-light item, or anything true of every car.
- Something the routine inspection already catches: fluid levels and leaks,
  brake-pad wear, injector bench tests, compression.

The test: *would a buyer learn this from a normal pre-purchase inspection
anyway?* If yes, it is noise. *Is it specific to this car's engine, gearbox or
mileage, and predictable from the ad?* If yes, it is why we exist.
