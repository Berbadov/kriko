What to check. Check for any history of timing chain issues or engine misfires. Inspect for signs of oil leaks or unusual noises from the timing chain area. Compare with the 2.0 TSI’s known ratchet mechanism failure history for context.

Wastegate rattle on 1.3 TCe (H5H)

Found with a diagnostic scan

The 1.3 TCe turbocharger's wastegate linkage rod can develop play over time, causing a metallic rattle and, if severe, boost leaks. This is a known issue specific to this engine code and can lead to reduced performance or turbo damage if left unattended.

What to check. During a test drive, listen for a metallic rattle from the turbocharger area, especially during boost or when revving. Also check for fault codes related to turbocharger position or boost pressure (e.g., P0243, P0244).

h5h (1.3 TCe) fuel pressure sensor wiring heat damage

Found with a diagnostic scan

The h5h engine (1.3 TCe) runs hot, which over time damages the fuel rail pressure sensor and its wiring. This can cause the engine warning light to come on with a false low fuel pressure error code, even though the fuel system works fine. Repair usually means replacing the sensor or fixing the harness.

What to check. At viewing, ask about past P0087 fault codes and inspect the fuel rail wiring connector for heat damage or corrosion. Consider a fuel pressure test while the engine is hot; if pressure is normal but P0087 appeared, the wiring/sensor is likely the culprit.

h5h fuel rail pressure sensor signal degradation

Found with a diagnostic scan

The fuel rail pressure sensor in the h5h engine can develop internal resistance changes over time. Even when actual fuel pressure is normal, the sensor sends wrong signals to the ECU, causing warning lights and drivability problems. This is a known age-related failure not easily spotted by a standard inspection.

What to check. At viewing, use an OBD scanner to check for fuel pressure faults (e.g., P0087); if possible, observe fuel pressure at idle and under load. Also inspect the sensor connector for corrosion or damage.

Minor

Carbon buildup on intake valves/ports (direct injection-specific)

Shown by the service history

Direct-injected engines like the 1.3 TCe H5Ht lack the air-fuel mixture cleaning effect of port injection, leading to carbon deposits on intake valves and ports. While rare to cause severe performance issues, it can restrict airflow and reduce efficiency over time.

What to check. Check for black carbon deposits on intake valves and ports during a visual inspection of the engine bay or during a valve cover removal. Listen for rough idling or reduced power, which may indicate restricted airflow due to carbon buildup.
