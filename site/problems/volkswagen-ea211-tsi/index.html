Volkswagen EA211 TSI: known problems

40 sourced reports, with what to check before you buy. Each one links to the page it was reported on.

Costly or dangerous

Balancer shaft drive gear failure in EA211 1.4 TFSI

Costly or dangerous Found with a diagnostic scan

In 1.4 TFSI EA211 engines, the balancer shaft drive gear can fail due to lubrication problems, causing vibrations and noise. Combined with low oil pressure, this can lead to severe engine damage, typically between 50,000 and 120,000 km. This is a specific, high-cost issue not easily detected in a standard pre-purchase inspection.

What to check. Listen for unusual engine vibration or noise, especially at idle. Check engine oil level and condition; verify oil pressure via OBD or gauge if possible. Review service history for regular oil changes with correct oil specification.

Cylinder head gasket failure due to neglected cooling system

Costly or dangerous Found with a diagnostic scan

Neglecting cooling system maintenance can lead to cylinder head gasket leaks, causing coolant level drops, white exhaust smoke, and overheating, especially in vehicles over 100,000 km.

What to check. Check for coolant level drops, white exhaust smoke, and overheating symptoms. Inspect cylinder head gasket condition if the vehicle exceeds 100,000 km.

EA211 1.4 TFSI piston ring excessive oil consumption

Costly or dangerous Found by looking

The 1.4 TFSI (EA211) engine can suffer from faulty piston rings that allow oil to burn in the cylinders. This leads to high oil consumption, especially on short trips, and can cause engine damage if ignored. It tends to appear between 30,000 and 80,000 km.

What to check. Ask for service records of oil top-ups and look for blue smoke from the exhaust, especially during cold start or acceleration. Perform a timed oil consumption test if possible; also check for oil leaks or low oil warnings in the dashboard history.

EA211 1.4 TFSI thermostat and coolant hose failures

Costly or dangerous Found with a diagnostic scan

The EA211 engine, including the 1.4 TFSI, can suffer from premature thermostat failure, causing the engine to reach operating temperature slowly or overheat. Coolant hoses may also crack, increasing the risk of leaks and serious engine damage. This issue tends to appear between 60,000 and 120,000 km, so a car around that mileage is particularly at risk.

What to check. During a test drive, let the engine idle and monitor the temperature gauge – it should reach mid-range steadily and stay stable. Inspect coolant hoses for cracks, bulges, or leaks, and check for any coolant smell. Use an OBD scanner to verify the engine reaches normal operating temperature within a few minutes; slow warm-up indicates a failing thermostat.

EA211 1.4 TFSI turbo oil leak and failure

Costly or dangerous Found with a diagnostic scan

The turbocharger on EA211 1.4 TFSI engines can develop oil seal leaks, causing blue-white exhaust smoke and eventual turbo failure. This tends to appear between 90,000–160,000 km, especially if the engine is shut off while the turbo is still hot.

What to check. During a cold start and after a highway run, check for blue/white smoke from the exhaust. Listen for turbo whine or abnormal spooling. Ask if the turbo has been replaced and whether the oil feed pipe was cleaned. Diagnostic codes such as P0299 (underboost) or P0243 may indicate turbo related issues.

Some first-generation EA211 1.4 TSI engines can burn oil at up to 0.5L per 1000 km due to premature piston ring wear, usually appearing between 60,000 and 120,000 km. If not checked, this can lead to severe engine damage.

What to check. Check oil level and history; look for blue exhaust smoke on startup or acceleration; consider a compression test and borescope inspection of cylinders.

EA211 Evo excessive oil consumption (ring wear or crankcase ventilation)

Costly or dangerous Found by looking

EA211 PCV valve failure causes oil starvation

Costly or dangerous Found by looking

A faulty PCV valve in the EA211 1.4 TSI lets oil vapor escape into the intake, reducing the oil level rapidly. Over time this can cause serious engine wear or failure, and is hard to spot without knowing what to look for.

What to check. Check the oil dipstick level and look for any signs of oil loss between services. Listen for a whistling or hissing noise from the valve cover area. Look for diagnostic trouble codes like P0171 or P0507, which may indicate a vacuum leak from the PCV system.

Costly or dangerous Shown by the service history

The EA211 engine uses a timing belt instead of a chain. If the belt breaks, it can destroy the engine. The belt must be replaced every 90,000 to 120,000 km, so check service records to confirm it was done.

What to check. Check service records for proof of timing belt replacement at or before 60,000 km. If no proof, assume it is due and budget for replacement. Inspect for any engine noise or running issues that might indicate belt wear.

Carbon buildup on intake valves in EA211 direct-injection engines

Worth checking Shown by the service history

The EA211 engine family (1.0, 1.4, 1.5 TSI/TFSI) uses direct injection, so fuel cannot wash over the intake valves. Over time, carbon deposits accumulate on the valves and intake tract, leading to rough idle, power loss, and increased fuel consumption. Periodic cleaning, such as walnut blasting, is needed, typically between 40,000 and 100,000 km.

What to check. Check service history for intake valve cleaning; if absent, ask for a borescope inspection of the intake valves. Rough idle or hesitation during test drive may indicate carbon buildup. Use this to negotiate price or require cleaning cost reduction.

EA211 TSI intake valve carbon buildup

Worth checking Shown by the service history

The direct-injection (TSI) variants of the EA211 engine can accumulate carbon deposits on intake valves over time. This happens because fuel is injected directly into the cylinder, not onto the intake valves, so the valves are not cleaned by fuel. The deposits can reduce airflow, causing rough idle, power loss, and increased fuel consumption. This issue is predictable based on engine type and mileage.

What to check. At viewing, ask if the intake valves have been cleaned (carbon blasting) and check service records. Consider a borescope inspection of the intake ports, which is not part of a standard check. Look for symptoms like rough idle, hesitation, or low power; DTC P2014/P2015 may indicate intake manifold flap issues, but not directly carbon. A compression test may show valve sealing problems, but it's routine. Carbon build-up is best confirmed via borescope.

Intake valve carbon buildup in EA211 1.4 TFSI

Worth checking Shown by the service history

Direct-injection engines like the EA211 do not spray fuel over the intake valves, so oil and combustion deposits accumulate over time. After roughly 80,000 km, this can cause rough cold starts, power loss, and uneven idling. Cleaning the valves requires labor-intensive walnut blasting or chemical treatment, which is a significant expense.

What to check. At the viewing, start the engine cold and listen for rough idle or hesitation. Ask the seller if the intake valves have ever been cleaned (walnut blasting). A borescope inspection of the intake ports and valves can directly reveal carbon buildup.

EA211 cold-start hesitation 'kangaroo' software issue

Minor

This engine family is known for a software-related hesitation or stuttering during cold starts, which can affect drivability. The issue often requires an ECU recalibration or software update from the dealer.
