Midea air conditioner smoking and catching fire at air outlet

SeriousSafety risk

AAStocks financial news (citing Chinese media) reports a Midea air conditioner that suddenly started smoking and caught fire; damage to the air outlet was severe, with smoke and fire marks on the ceiling and wall. Midea Group responded that it was actively addressing the issue. This is a Chinese-market incident, not TR/EU, but documents a fire event on a Midea AC.

What to checkA documented fire/smoke incident on a Midea AC exists; buyers should ensure proper installation, keep the air outlet clear, and monitor the unit for unusual smells or smoke.

Reported onaastocks.com

Midea/Jeriyan split AC control board and fan motor burn-out after seasonal shutdown

Serious

Turkish complaint site (sikayetvar.com) documents a Midea-made Jeriyan 48 BTU salon-type split unit bought July 2025, used two months, then on restart in June 2026 it failed. Authorized service said the control board and fan motor had burned and replaced them; afterwards the unit repeatedly tripped the building's circuit breaker. Multiple service visits (4–5) did not resolve the problem; the manufacturer's customer service stopped responding.

What to checkA buyer should verify the local service network and warranty enforcement before purchase; this owner reports a burned control board and fan motor, then persistent electrical faults (breaker tripping) that service could not fix, leaving the unit unusable.

Reported onsikayetvar.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Worth checking2

Midea Porta Split error EL 0C, unrepairable due to missing spare parts

To check

German heise.de forum post: an owner's Midea Porta Split (bought at OBI 1.5 years earlier, under warranty) showed error EL 0C and stopped cooling. OBI said the unit could not be repaired because no spare parts exist, and offered a full refund of the €979 purchase price instead. This documents a spare-part availability failure on a Midea split-type unit in the EU market.

What to checkSpare-part scarcity is a documented EU-market risk for Midea portable splits; a buyer should check parts availability and service support before purchase, since a failed unit may be unrepairable even under warranty.

Reported onheise.de

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Midea error code E6 indicates refrigerant leak detection

To check

Midea's official Spanish installation/service manual for the Minisplit Aurora Xtreme Inverter lists error code E6 as 'Se detectó una fuga de refrigerante' (refrigerant leak detected). This is the manufacturer's own documentation confirming the unit can detect and report refrigerant leaks.

What to checkIf a Midea split displays E6, the manufacturer's documentation indicates a refrigerant leak; the unit should be serviced by a professional, as refrigerant leaks are a safety and efficiency hazard.

Reported onmidea.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Minor1

Midea ED error code indicates outdoor unit malfunction

Minor

A Turkish Midea-focused blog explains that the Midea ED error code generally indicates a malfunction in the outdoor unit or activation of system protection mode; the unit shuts itself down to protect the compressor and other main components. Causes listed include indoor/outdoor communication issues, grid voltage fluctuations, compressor overheating, outdoor sensor faults, and outdoor fan motor problems.

What to checkThe ED error code points to the outdoor unit; a buyer seeing this code should expect a professional diagnosis of the outdoor communication board, compressor, sensors, or fan motor.

Reported onmidea-klima.com
