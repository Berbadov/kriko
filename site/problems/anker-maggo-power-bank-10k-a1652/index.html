Anker officially recalls A1652 MagGo Power Bank due to fire risk from lithium-ion battery defect

SeriousSafety risk

This is Anker's official recall page confirming the A1652 is affected by a manufacturing defect in lithium-ion batteries that can cause overheating, melting of plastic components, smoke, and fire. The recall covers units from January 3, 2024 to September 17, 2024, with an expanded recall launched in June 2025.

What to checkIf you own an A1652, immediately stop using it, check the serial number on Anker's recall page, and dispose of it at a facility that accepts lithium-ion batteries. Do not throw it in the trash or recycling bins.

Reported onanker.com

CPSC recalls ~481,000 Anker power banks including A1652 after 33 fire/explosion reports

SeriousSafety risk

This is the CPSC's official recall notice (Spanish version) dated September 18, 2025, covering approximately 481,000 units across five models including the A1652. The company received 33 reports of fire and explosion incidents, 4 minor burn injuries, and 1 report of significant property damage.

What to checkThis is a formal US government recall. If you own an A1652, stop using it immediately, verify the serial number on Anker's recall page, and contact Anker for a full refund or gift card. Do not dispose of it in regular trash or recycling.

Reported oncpsc.gov

Documented fire incident: A1652 caught fire while charging in Japan on April 10, 2026

SeriousSafety risk

This is a Japanese news report from Weekly ASCII (May 29, 2026) documenting that Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency reported a fire incident on April 10, 2026 involving the A1652 model. The product caught fire while charging, burning the product and surrounding area. The cause is under investigation.

What to checkThis is a documented actual fire incident involving the A1652 model, not just a theoretical risk. The unit was being charged when it caught fire. This underscores the real-world fire risk of this product.

Reported onweekly.ascii.jp

To check
