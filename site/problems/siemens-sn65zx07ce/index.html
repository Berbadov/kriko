Costly or dangerous

Pump failure after one year on Siemens SN65ZX07CE

A Czech user review on Alza.cz (rated 4.0/5 from 6 users) reports the pump died after one year. The reviewer notes that newer pumps from Siemens and Bosch are designed for low consumption and noise but are more sensitive and fail faster. This is a verified-purchase owner report of a major component failure.

What to check. The circulation pump is a critical, expensive part. If it fails after the warranty period, expect a significant repair bill. Consider extended warranty coverage for this model.

Reported on: m.alza.cz

Siemens official: E31/E15/E3100 = water protection system activated

Siemens' own German support page states E31 (along with E15, F15, E3100) means the water protection system is activated. This corroborates the owner-reported E31 on the SN65ZX07CE and confirms it is a water-leak safety trigger, not a software glitch.

What to check. Treat E31 as a potential leak. Siemens explicitly advises against self-repair for health/safety reasons: close the water tap, switch the unit off, and book a service visit. Expect a technician diagnosis rather than a user fix.

Reported on: siemens-home.bsh-group.com

Worth checking

Bearing failure causing noise on Siemens SN65ZX07CE

In a Swedish forum thread about a noise issue on a SN65ZX07CE, another member (sessions32) reports having the same problem and that it was a broken bearing that was repaired under warranty. This is an owner-reported mechanical failure requiring warranty repair.

What to check. If the unit makes unusual noises, a bearing may be failing. This was covered under warranty for the reporting owner; check your warranty status early.

Reported on: byggahus.se

Minor

Detergent dosing leaves tablets undissolved; touch control delays; cutlery basket fixation

Onliner.by's AI-generated summary of verified reviews for the exact SN65ZX07CE lists recurring minor complaints: the detergent dosing system sometimes leaves tablets undissolved, the touch control can respond with delay or error, the cutlery basket fixation is poorly designed, some programs run long, and plastic items stay wet even after intensive drying.

What to check. These are low-severity usability annoyances rather than failures. If tablets come out undissolved, check loading and the detergent dispenser flap; if the touch panel misbehaves, it may be a sensor sensitivity issue. None of these should stop a purchase, but they set expectations on drying of plastics and program length.

Reported on: catalog.onliner.by

Detergent tablet sometimes left undissolved

Onliner.by aggregates verified owner reviews and notes the detergent dispensing system sometimes leaves tablets undissolved. This is a recurring, user-visible quality issue.

What to check. If tablets are left undissolved, check the detergent dispenser flap and ensure the tablet is placed correctly; this may be a design limitation rather than a user error.

Reported on: catalog.onliner.by

Poorly thought-out cutlery basket fixation system

Onliner.by notes the cutlery basket fixation system is poorly designed and causes inconvenience. This is a usability complaint.

What to check. The cutlery basket may not stay firmly in place; this is a minor ergonomic issue.

Reported on: catalog.onliner.by

Sensitive touch control can trigger with delay or error

Onliner.by notes the sensitive touch control can trigger with delay or error. This is a usability annoyance rather than a critical failure.

What to check. Expect occasional touch control lag or mis-triggers; this is a minor annoyance, not a reason to avoid the model.

Reported on: catalog.onliner.by

Water drops remain on plastic dishes even after intensive drying

Onliner.by reports water drops remain on plastic dishes even after intensive drying. This is a common limitation of dishwasher drying systems with plastic items.

What to check. Plastic items may come out wet despite the Zeolith drying technology; this is a known limitation, not a defect.

Reported on: catalog.onliner.by
