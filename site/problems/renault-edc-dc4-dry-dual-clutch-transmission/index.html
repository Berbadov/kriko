DPF failures on 1.5 dCi (dc4)

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The 1.5 dCi diesel engine in the Clio Mk4 has known DPF issues, especially in cars used mainly for short journeys. This causes the filter to clog, leading to loss of power, warning lights, and expensive repairs. A standard pre-purchase inspection won't catch this unless the car is actively showing symptoms.

What to checkCheck for DPF-related trouble codes (e.g., P242F, P2002). Look for soot accumulation or excessive pressure differential readings on a scan tool. Ask about driving history; short trips raise risk. Inspect for signs of forced regeneration or previous DPF replacement.

Reported onmycaros.com

EDC Dual-Clutch Gearbox Jerking & Premature Clutch Wear

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The EDC dual-clutch automatic gearbox in early Renault Clio Mk4 models (2013-2015) is prone to jerky operation, juddering, and accelerated clutch wear, likely due to design or calibration flaws in the early implementation.

What to checkCheck for jerky shifts, unusual noises during gear changes, and verify full service history for the EDC gearbox. Test drive to confirm smooth acceleration and no clutch-related hesitation.

Reported onmycaros.com

EDC control unit failure in DC4 dual-clutch transmission

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The EDC (Electronic Dual Clutch) control unit is the brain of the DC4 gearbox. When it fails, the car may hesitate, jerk, or lose reverse gear. Repair or replacement is costly and not detectable by a simple inspection.

What to checkCheck for 'gearbox fault' warnings, test all gear changes including reverse, and ask for service history showing EDC unit replacement. If possible, read DTCs for P17xx codes.

Reported onrenaultelektronikservisi.com.tr

EDC dual clutch disc wear causes shudder and slipping

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The EDC dual clutch discs wear over time, causing the engine to rev without acceleration, shuddering, jerking, and metallic knocking sounds during gear changes. This is especially noticeable when starting uphill and shifting from 1st to 2nd gear.

What to checkDuring a test drive, check for shudder or slipping during take-off and 1st-2nd gear changes, especially on an incline. Listen for metallic knocking during shifts. Also check maintenance history for clutch/transmission oil changes.

Reported onparcaekseni.com

Failing ignition coils and catastrophic engine failure due to neglected timing belt

SeriousShown by the service history

The 1.2 16v 75 (D4F 740) engine suffers from ignition coil failures and, if the timing belt is not replaced at specified intervals, catastrophic engine failure can occur.

What to checkInspect ignition coils for wear or failure, verify timing belt condition and service history, and listen for unusual engine noises (e.g., misfires, rattling).

Premature clutch wear and release bearing failure in DC4 transmission

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The Megane 3 manual transmission (code DC4) has a chronic issue with premature clutch wear and hydraulic release bearing failure, causing rough shifts and noise. This is a known design weakness that can be predicted by mileage and driving style.

What to checkDuring test drive, check for clutch slipping, rough gear engagement, and squealing from the release bearing. Listen for unusual noise when pressing the clutch pedal.

Timing chain stretch and failure in 0.9 TCe (H4Bt) engine

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The 0.9 TCe 90 engine (H4Bt 400) is known for timing chain stretch around 150,000 km, which can cause catastrophic engine failure. Oil consumption is often an early warning sign. This is a serious, expensive issue specific to this engine code.

What to checkListen for rattling noise from the timing chain area on cold start, check engine oil level and any history of oil consumption complaints. Look for fault codes like P0011 or P0016 (if available) and verify whether the timing chain has been replaced.

Transmission actuator or gear shift motor faults (dc4)

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The evidence indicates that dc4's actuator/gear shift motors can cause delayed or harsh shifts, or even complete failure. These faults can also affect the transmission computer and clutch, leading to a costly repair. This is a specific, predictable transmission issue a buyer should know about.

What to checkCheck for an EDC warning light, test gear shifts for delay or harshness, and read transmission fault codes (e.g., actuator errors) during a diagnostic scan.

Transmission control unit software failure causes gear loss

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The transmission control unit (TCU) can develop software or calibration faults that cause the car to lose certain gears or fail to shift properly. This can happen after 30-40 minutes of stop-and-go traffic and is not visible at a cold start, making it hard to detect without a thorough test drive.

What to checkDuring a test drive, include stop-and-go traffic for at least 30 minutes. Monitor if all gears engage, especially odd/even gear selection. Use an OBD scanner to check for transmission DTCs (e.g., gear ratio errors, clutch position faults).

Reported onparcaekseni.com

dc4 Getrag transmission mechatronics wear causing gear change issues

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The Getrag automatic transmission in this car can develop worn mechatronics (the electronic control unit that shifts gears) and software glitches, especially in early examples. This can cause rough or delayed gear changes. Replacing the mechatronics is an expensive repair, so it's worth checking the transmission behavior.

What to checkTake a test drive and feel for jerky or hesitant gear changes, especially when cold. Check for transmission warning lights or codes (e.g., P0700, P17xx). Ask if the mechatronics has been replaced or updated under warranty. Note that a routine inspection won't easily detect internal mechatronics wear without a diagnostic scan.

Reported onautotrader.co.za

dc4 transmission lock-up from neglected fluid changes

Serious

What to checkCheck the service history for transmission fluid and filter changes at the recommended intervals. Look for any dashboard warnings or shifting issues during a test drive.

To checkNoticed on a test drive

EDC dual-clutch transmissions have pressure plates (clutch discs) that wear out faster than those in conventional automatics. This leads to premature clutch failure and requires more frequent replacement, typically noticeable after a certain mileage.

What to checkDuring the test drive, check for clutch slip, judder, or hesitation during gear changes. Ask for service records showing whether the clutch has been replaced, and consider the vehicle's mileage and age.

PMDC Motor Friction Brush Wear and Failure

To check

Friction brushes in PMDC motors wear down over time, leading to poor electrical contact with the commutator, which can cause the motor to fail to start or operate inefficiently. Severe wear or damage necessitates replacement.

What to checkCheck the brushes for wear beyond half their original length and inspect for cracks or chipping. Ensure they maintain proper contact with the commutator.
