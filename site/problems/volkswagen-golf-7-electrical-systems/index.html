Costly or dangerous

: faulty door crash sensors may disable airbags

A recall has been issued for defective crash sensors in the front doors that can affect airbag and restraint systems. If not repaired, these sensors could prevent airbags from deploying in a crash, creating a serious safety risk. Buyers should make sure the recall work has been done on the Golf 7 they are considering.

What to check. Check the vehicle's VIN against Volkswagen's recall database to confirm the crash sensor recall has been performed. Ask service records for proof of recall completion.

Reported on: whatcar.com

E-call failure in online control unit

Found by looking

The e-call system may fail to contact emergency services after a crash due to a software fault in the Online Control Unit. This is a known recall issue, but if not fixed, it leaves occupants without automatic emergency assistance.

What to check. Check if the dealership has completed the recall for the e-call system. If possible, test the e-call button (with permission) and verify the system status via diagnostic scan.

Reported on: whatcar.com

Golf 7 electrical: Front Assist phantom braking

Found with a diagnostic scan

Some Golf 7 models have a fault where the Front Assist emergency braking system activates for no reason, causing sudden unintended braking. This is a safety-critical electronic issue that may require a software update or deactivating the system. It is not detectable during a routine pre-purchase inspection.

What to check. Ask for the service history and any software updates for the Front Assist system. During the test drive, note any sudden braking or warning messages. Inspect the radar sensor and windshield camera for damage or misalignment.

Reported on: what-breaks.com

Golf7 DSG hesitation and rough shifting

Found with a diagnostic scan

The DSG is a dual-clutch automatic gearbox. Hesitation or jerky shifts can indicate problems with the mechatronic control unit or degraded transmission fluid. Without proper servicing, this may lead to expensive repairs or complete transmission failure.

What to check. During a test drive, accelerate smoothly from a standstill and note any hesitation or jerky shifts. Check the service history for DSG oil changes (VW recommends every 60,000 km). If possible, scan fault codes; look for mechatronic-related codes such as P17B4.

Reported on: bumper.co

infotainment errors disable parking sensors and cross-traffic alerts

Found by looking

The infotainment system in a Golf 7 can show error messages that unexpectedly turn off parking sensors and cross-traffic alerts. This is a safety issue because these driver assistance features may fail without warning. A buyer should know this specific electrical problem.

What to check. During the viewing, start the engine and check the infotainment screen for error messages. Test the parking sensors by engaging reverse and moving near an obstacle, and verify cross-traffic alerts if possible. A diagnostic scan for fault codes in the infotainment and driver assistance modules is recommended.

Reported on: lemberglaw.com

Worth checking

AdBlue system issues in diesel models

Found with a diagnostic scan

Diesel owners have reported problems with the AdBlue system, which is critical for emissions control and may lead to drivability issues or failure to meet emissions standards.

What to check. Check for warning lights related to the AdBlue system. Verify if the system has been serviced recently and if there are any error codes stored in the vehicle's computer.

Reported on: whatcar.com

DSG gearbox software issues cause erratic shifting

The DSG automatic gearbox in the Golf 7.5 can have software glitches that cause rough or unexpected gear changes. A software update might fix it temporarily, but the problem can come back, affecting drivability and potentially leading to costly repairs.

What to check. During a test drive, pay attention to hesitation, jerky shifts, or unexpected gear changes, especially in stop-and-go traffic. Ask if the DSG software has been updated recently, and consider having a dealer scan for transmission fault codes (e.g., related to the mechatronic unit).

Reported on: bumper.co

Infotainment and SOS failures in Golf 7 electrical system

Found by looking

Owners report that the touchscreen infotainment system often freezes or fails completely, and the SOS emergency call function can trigger warning lights and require expensive repairs after software updates. This is an electrical/electronic issue that may not be evident during a short test drive.

What to check. Test the infotainment screen for lag/freezing, check for any SOS warning lights, and verify the SOS system responds to a test call if possible. During a diagnostic scan, look for fault codes in the infotainment and emergency call modules.

Reported on: whatcar.com

ignition coil/plug misfires

Found with a diagnostic scan

Known issue in Golf 7 (2013–2017) where ignition coils and spark plugs can fail, causing misfires. This can lead to rough running, reduced fuel economy, and if ignored, possible engine damage.

What to check. Ask for a diagnostic scan and look for misfire codes (P0300-P0308). Also check if coils and plugs have been replaced recently.

Reported on: bumper.co
