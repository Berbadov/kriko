CPSC recall covers PowerCore 10000 model A1263 only, not the A1229 Slim

Minor

The CPSC recall of about 1,158,000 units is limited to model A1263 (PowerCore 10000), which is a different model from the subject A1229 (PowerCore Slim 10000). A buyer should not assume their A1229 is covered by this recall.

What to checkCheck the model number printed on the bottom of the unit. Only A1263 units with qualifying serial numbers are recalled; the A1229 is outside this recall's scope.

Reported oncpsc.gov

No direct recall or serious accident report confirmed for the A1229

Minor

This Japanese-language analysis of the A1229 states that, as of the analysis period, no direct recall instruction or serious accident report has been confirmed for the A1229 (and A1244). It notes the sibling A1263 recall of 2025 affected brand trust but not this model itself.

What to checkNo documented recall or serious accident report is tied to the A1229. The 2025 CPSC recall applies to the A1263, a different model, so it should not be treated as covering the A1229.

Reported onnote.com

No documented safety event found tied specifically to the A1229

Minor

This finding came from the query 'CPSC Anker PowerCore 10000 A1229 recall'. The recall is for the PowerCore 10000 (A1263); my searches for A1229-specific fire, overheating, or swollen-battery events returned no documented incident tied to that model.

What to checkNo recall or reported fire/overheating incident was found for the A1229 specifically. The documented 2025 recall applies to the A1263, a different model. If you are deciding whether to buy the A1229, the known safety event in this category is the A1263 recall, not this unit.

Reported onhowtogeek.com
