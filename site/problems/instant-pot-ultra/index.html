Costly or dangerous

Instant Pot Ultra Series named in class action over defective lid-locking assembly

The Ultra Series is explicitly named in a federal class action alleging the lid-locking mechanism can fail during normal use, allowing the lid to open while contents are under pressure, causing scalding eruptions. This is a documented, litigated safety defect affecting the Ultra model.

What to check. If you own an Instant Pot Ultra, never force the lid open and always verify the float valve has dropped before opening. The lawsuit alleges the safety mechanism can fail even in normal use.

Reported on: classactionlawsuithelp.com

Worth checking

Instant Pot Ultra manual documents 'Ovht' overheating warning and pressure failure mode

The official Ultra manual documents an 'Ovht' (overheating) warning where the cooker reduces heating temperature and may fail to reach cooking pressure, potentially caused by starch deposits blocking heat dissipation from the inner pot bottom.

What to check. If the Ultra shows an Ovht warning, stop cooking and inspect the bottom of the inner pot for residue buildup. The unit may fail to reach pressure, meaning food may not be safely cooked.

Reported on: manualslib.de

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Instant Pot Ultra official troubleshooting documents temperature sensor error codes C1/C2/C5

The official Instant Pot troubleshooting document explicitly covers the Ultra model and lists C1/C2/C5 error codes indicating faulty temperature sensors, requiring contacting customer care. This indicates a known electrical failure mode.

What to check. If the display shows C1, C2, or C5 codes, the unit requires service; the official guidance is to contact Instant Pot customer care rather than attempt DIY repair.

Reported on: m.media-amazon.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27
