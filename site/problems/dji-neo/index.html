DJI Neo: known problems

3 sourced reports, with what to check before you buy. Each one links to the page it was reported on.

Worth checking

CVE-2026-78321: HTTP media server DoS blocks QuickTransfer (Neo)

INCIBE's advisory CVE-2026-78321 documents a denial-of-service vulnerability in the Neo's HTTP media server (firmware up to 01.00.0400) that blocks QuickTransfer photo/video retrieval, fixed only by a firmware update.

What to check. Keep the Neo's firmware current; the documented fix for this vulnerability is a vendor firmware update, and an unpatched unit can have its media transfer disrupted.

Reported on: incibe.es

DJI bulletin: gimbal moves erratically (Neo series)

DJI's official support bulletin lists the Neo series as an applicable product for erratic gimbal movement and gimbal hardware errors, with gimbal calibration in DJI Fly as the first fix.

What to check. If the gimbal moves erratically or the app warns, try gimbal auto-calibration in DJI Fly first; if it persists the bulletin points to a gimbal hardware error needing service.

Reported on: support.dji.com

Gimbal rubber bumpers break after a crash

On the iFixit teardown page an owner reports the gimbal's rubber bumpers break in a crash; another owner notes the frame, connectors, motors and gyroscope are glued in place, making DIY repair extremely difficult.

What to check. The Neo is not user-serviceable: internal parts are glued, so a crash that breaks the gimbal bumpers or frame effectively means replacement or a service-center repair, which can cost close to a new unit.

Reported on: zh.ifixit.com
