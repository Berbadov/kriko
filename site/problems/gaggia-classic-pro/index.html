Costly or dangerous1

Three-way solenoid valve clogs with scale, stalling flow through the group head

Serious

The manufacturer's own service guide lists the three-way solenoid valve clogging as the leading cause of flow problems on the Classic Pro.

What to checkExpect the group head to lose flow over time as scale builds up; cleaning the solenoid valve requires opening the machine (roughly 30 minutes). Buyers should plan regular descaling (about every 90 days) or budget for a solenoid service.

Reported onsupport.wholelattelove.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Worth checking1

Vibratory pump failure causes intermittent brew-flow delays

To check

A Home-Barista owner replaced the pump after noticing intermittent delays from hitting brew to achieving flow through the puck.

What to checkPump failure shows up as slow or delayed flow; replacing it means opening the machine and risks breaking the plastic pump-mount tower. Budget for a pump service if flow delays appear.

Reported onhome-barista.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Minor2

Over-pressure valve ships set at about 12 bar instead of 9

Minor

A Home-Barista member reports the OPV is factory-set at 12 bar and that lowering it to 9-10 bar improved shots.

What to checkThe factory OPV setting is higher than the usual 9-bar espresso standard, which can make shots run fast or bitter; adjusting the OPV is a known first tweak.

Reported onhome-barista.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Steam valve o-ring leaks, spritzing water at the rear of the group head

Minor

The same Home-Barista owner traced a spray leak to the o-ring under the steam valve.

What to checkA small leak at the rear of the group head is often the steam-valve o-ring, a cheap part that is easy to replace once the top is off.
