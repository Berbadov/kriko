ZOTAC RTX 3060 Twin Edge OC melted PSU-to-GPU wires (owner report)

Serious

Two-star Amazon owner review of the ZOTAC Gaming GeForce RTX 3060 Twin Edge OC 12GB. The owner reports the first unit smoked the PSU and that plugging the card in melted the wires from PSU to GPU, while a second card and a swapped PSU worked fine. Single anecdote on one AIB variant, so treat as a possible unit-level defect rather than a series-wide issue.

What to checkA melted PSU-to-GPU power cable is a fire hazard; if it happens, stop use immediately, replace the PSU/cables, and RMA the card. This is one owner's report on a single variant, not evidence of a series-wide connector problem like the RTX 4090's.

Reported onamazon.sg

RTX 3060 PCI-E power connector broke off; insufficient soldering confirmed as warranty case

To check

German owner forum (Computerbase) thread about a PCI-E connector that broke off an RTX 3060. The user reports the manufacturer accepted it as a warranty case, stating the card was insufficiently soldered and that it was not the first card with this problem.

What to checkIf the power connector on the card is loose or the card fails to power on, treat it as a manufacturing defect and pursue warranty/RMA rather than attempting a solder repair; the seller acknowledged this is a known recurring issue.

Reported oncomputerbase.de

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

RTX 3060 black screen after Windows auto-installs driver; card no longer detected

To check

French-language Microsoft Q&A thread (EU market) where an RTX 3060 owner reports that after Windows auto-installs the driver and the PC restarts, the card is no longer detected and the screen stays black, while the card works perfectly on Linux. The conflict is between Windows' auto-installed driver and the NVIDIA driver.

What to checkOn Windows, the auto-installed driver can leave the 3060 undetected with a black screen after reboot; owners resolve this by fully removing the driver with DDU in Safe Mode before installing the NVIDIA driver. Expect this as a possible setup hurdle, not a hardware fault.

Reported onlearn.microsoft.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

RTX 3060 recurring crashes in games tied to nvlddmkm.sys 0x116 TDR failure

To check

Microsoft Q&A thread from an RTX 3060 owner reporting constant in-game crashes with a VIDEO_TDR_FAILURE (0x116) pointing at nvlddmkm.sys. The user tried DDU clean installs of multiple driver versions without success; only lowering GPU clock and memory speed in Afterburner stopped the crashes.

What to checkIf a 3060 crashes in games with a 0x116/nvlddmkm.sys error, a clean driver reinstall may not fix it; undervolting/undervolting the clock and memory is the workaround owners report. This is a driver-level quirk, not a hardware failure, but it costs troubleshooting time.

Reported onlearn.microsoft.com

Newest linked source retrieved: 2026-10-27

Minor1

MSI RTX 3060 VENTUS 2X OC reaches ~90 °C under load after two years, even after repasting

Minor

PcComponentes community thread (EU) where an owner of an MSI GeForce RTX 3060 VENTUS 2X OC reports the card started overheating to ~90 °C under load after two years, with fans at maximum, and that replacing thermal paste did not help. This is variant-specific and age-related rather than a series-wide defect.

What to checkA 3060 running hot after a couple of years may need new thermal pads and proper heatsink re-seating, not just paste; on this MSI Ventus variant, repasting alone did not fix it. Check thermals before buying a used card of this age.

Reported onpccomponentes.fr
