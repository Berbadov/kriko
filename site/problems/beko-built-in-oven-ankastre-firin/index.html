Beko BFC 431 B built-in oven door glass shattered spontaneously while cold and off

Serious

Owner reports the door glass of a nearly-new Beko BFC 431 B built-in oven shattered spontaneously while the oven was cold, closed and unused, when touched with water. Beko's service quoted 1,650 TL for the replacement and declined to cover it. This is a spontaneous, unpredictable failure with injury risk and out-of-pocket cost.

What to checkIf you own or are considering a Beko built-in oven, be aware of documented spontaneous door-glass shattering reports; check whether your unit is within warranty and push for coverage, as Beko service has reportedly charged owners for replacement glass even on near-new units.

Reported onsikayetvar.com

Beko built-in oven middle door glass shattered after normal use at 200°C

Serious

Owner reports the middle door glass of a Beko built-in oven shattered about 5 minutes after normal use at 200°C (well below the oven's 250°C maximum), scattering glass on the floor. The service technician attributed it to user error and charged for the glass. This indicates the failure occurs within normal operating parameters and owners bear the cost.

What to checkTreat Beko built-in oven door glass as a documented failure point even under normal use; if it shatters, expect the service to blame user error and charge for replacement.

Reported onsikayetvar.com

Worth checking1

Beko ovens were notified through EU RAPEX system (notification 0354/09)

To check

A UK parliamentary written answer confirms Beko ovens were the subject of a RAPEX notification (0354/09), but the record does not state the nature of the fault, the models affected, or whether built-in ovens were included. This is a weak but real recall signal.

What to checkA formal EU rapid-alert notification exists for Beko ovens, but without fault details or model scope it cannot be acted on directly; treat it as a reason to check Beko's recall pages for your specific model.

Reported ontheyworkforyou.com
