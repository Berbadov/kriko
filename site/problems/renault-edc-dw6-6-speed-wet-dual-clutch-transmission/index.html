DW6 009 delayed engagement from low hydraulic pressure

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

This transmission can hesitate before engaging drive or reverse because internal hydraulic pressure is too low. It is a known weakness of the DW6 009 and may indicate wear or a failing pump/seal. Repairing it often involves expensive transmission work.

What to checkDuring the test drive, shift from Park to Drive and Reverse, noting any delay before engagement. Check transmission fluid level and condition (smell, color). Use a diagnostic tool to scan for transmission pressure or solenoid fault codes.

DW6 009 mechatronics failure: leaks, solenoids, electronics

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The automatic transmission's mechatronics unit controls shifting. It can develop internal leaks, electronic faults, or stuck solenoids, causing erratic shifting or total failure. This is a known issue specific to the DW6 009 and is expensive to repair.

What to checkCheck transmission oil condition and level; scan for mechatronics-related fault codes; test drive for harsh or delayed shifts.

DW6 009 transmission case seal oil leaks

Serious

The DW6 009 gearbox can develop oil leaks from its case seals, which may drop fluid level and eventually damage the transmission if ignored. This is a specific failure mode of this transmission code, not a general car issue.

What to checkInspect the underside for oil leaks around the gearbox casing, check transmission fluid level and condition, and look for fresh oil drips after a test drive.

DW6 EDC gearbox loses even gears due to dual clutch failure

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The DW6 dual-clutch gearbox can develop a specific fault where even gears (2,4,6) stop engaging while odd gears still work. This points to wear or damage in the dual clutch assembly, which is expensive to repair. A buyer should test all gear changes or check for transmission error codes.

What to checkDuring test drive, accelerate through all gears and ensure even gears engage. Check for transmission DTCs such as clutch or gear position errors. Also listen for abnormal noises or jerking.

DW6 EDC transmission loses even gears after clutch failure

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The DW6 automatic gearbox uses a dual-clutch system. A specific failure can cause one clutch to stop working, which results in the car only engaging odd gears (1-3-5) while even gears (2-4-6) are lost. This is a serious transmission problem that is expensive to repair and not detectable from a standard inspection without driving.

What to checkDuring the test drive, accelerate through all gears and confirm smooth engagement of 2nd, 4th and 6th. If you notice missing gears, slipping, or jerky shifts, suspect a dual-clutch failure. Have a transmission specialist read fault codes and inspect clutch operation.

DW6 clutch pack wear from oil seal leaks

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

Oil leaks through the seal allow clutch slip and accelerate wear of the clutch pack. This is a known issue for the DW6 gearbox, leading to costly transmission repairs. A buyer should check for oil leaks and clutch behavior during a test drive.

What to checkInspect for oil leaks around the gearbox, especially near the clutch area. Test drive for clutch slip (revs rising without speed). Check transmission fault codes and service history for clutch replacements.

DW6 dual-clutch failure loses even gears

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The DW6 automatic gearbox uses two clutches to engage odd and even gears. If one clutch fails, the car will refuse to engage even gears (2nd, 4th, 6th) and may enter limp mode. This is a known, expensive repair, so buyers should test all gears during a test drive.

What to checkDuring test drive, accelerate through all gears, especially check 2nd, 4th, and 6th gear engagement. Look for jerking, hesitation, or warning lights. Ask for gearbox service history and check for any common DTCs like P284A or P284B related to clutch failure.

DW6 solenoid contamination from clutch wear debris

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

In this automatic gearbox, normal clutch wear produces fine particles that can contaminate the solenoid valves. Over time this causes shifting problems, which can be costly to repair. It is a known failure pattern specific to this gearbox model.

What to checkDuring a test drive, pay attention to hesitant or jerky shifting, especially when cold. If possible, scan the transmission control unit for fault codes related to solenoid or shift pressure.
