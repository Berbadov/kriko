Dyson AM05: known problems

3 sourced reports, with what to check before you buy. Each one links to the page it was reported on.

Costly or dangerous

Dyson identifies the printed circuit board short as the defect and installs a protective part

Dyson's Japanese notice names the concrete part: an electrical short on the printed circuit board, fixed by fitting a protective board component.

What to check. The defect is on the PCB; the recall remedy is a free engineering fix (protective part) or replacement, so an affected unit should not be used until fixed.

Reported on: dyson.co.jp

Dyson voluntarily recalls all AM04/AM05 heaters after short-circuits burned and melted internal parts

This is Dyson's own official recall site, confirming the voluntary recall and free fix.

What to check. Register the unit's serial number at dysonrecall.com to get the free fix or replacement; this is the manufacturer's own remedy.

Reported on: dysonrecall.com

Owner reports AM05 overheated, tripped circuit breaker and blew out wall socket

A verified-buyer review on a retailer site reports a real overheating incident: burning smell, tripped breaker, blown wall socket after the second use.

What to check. This is an owner-reported incident consistent with the documented short-circuit hazard; treat any burning smell or tripped breaker as a reason to stop using and unplug the unit immediately.

Reported on: appliancesonline.com.au
