Costly or dangerous3

Dyson identifies the printed circuit board short as the defect and installs a protective part

Serious

Dyson's Japanese notice names the concrete part: an electrical short on the printed circuit board, fixed by fitting a protective board component.

What to checkThe defect is on the PCB; the recall remedy is a free engineering fix (protective part) or replacement, so an affected unit should not be used until fixed.

Reported ondyson.co.jp

Dyson voluntarily recalls all AM04/AM05 heaters after short-circuits burned and melted internal parts

Serious

This is Dyson's own official recall site, confirming the voluntary recall and free fix.

What to checkRegister the unit's serial number at dysonrecall.com to get the free fix or replacement; this is the manufacturer's own remedy.

Reported ondysonrecall.com

Owner reports AM05 overheated, tripped circuit breaker and blew out wall socket

Serious

A verified-buyer review on a retailer site reports a real overheating incident: burning smell, tripped breaker, blown wall socket after the second use.

What to checkThis is an owner-reported incident consistent with the documented short-circuit hazard; treat any burning smell or tripped breaker as a reason to stop using and unplug the unit immediately.

Reported onappliancesonline.com.au
