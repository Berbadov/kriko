Clutch temperature sensor G509 failure in DQ250

Costly or dangerous Noticed on a test drive

A sensor that monitors clutch temperature can fail, causing the transmission to overheat without warning. This can lead to costly clutch and mechatronic damage. It is a specific risk for DQ250 gearboxes.

What to check. At viewing, scan the transmission control module for fault code P1740 (18148) and test clutch temperature readings if possible. Ask for maintenance history of the mechatronic and clutch.

Costly or dangerous Found with a diagnostic scan

Moisture or oxidation on the mechatronic control unit can cause solenoid electrical failure, leading to erratic shifting or limp mode activation.

What to check. Inspect mechatronic unit for corrosion or moisture ingress. Check for erratic shifting, limp mode, or no reverse gear engagement. Use VCDS to verify solenoid signal integrity.

Costly or dangerous Noticed on a test drive

The DQ250 DSG gearbox has a known software problem that can cause the engine to stall when braking, especially in stop-and-go traffic. This is due to improper torque management and clutch engagement. It can be a safety concern and may not be found in a standard inspection.

What to check. During a test drive, simulate stop-and-go traffic and brake gently to see if the engine stalls. Ask whether the gearbox software has been updated or if any relevant recalls/service bulletins exist.

DQ250 DSG mechatronic hydraulic pressure failure

The DQ250 DSG gearbox has a mechatronic unit that controls hydraulic pressure for gear shifts. This unit can develop chronic pressure regulation problems, causing limp mode and poor gear engagement. What to check. At viewing, test drive and feel for jerky or delayed shifts. Check for transmission warning lights and use an OBD scanner to look for stored fault codes related to hydraulic pressure regulation (e.g., pressure or solenoid codes). Also check the DSG fluid condition and service history.
