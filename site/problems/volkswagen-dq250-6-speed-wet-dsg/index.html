Clutch temperature sensor G509 failure in DQ250

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

A sensor that monitors clutch temperature can fail, causing the transmission to overheat without warning. This can lead to costly clutch and mechatronic damage. It is a specific risk for DQ250 gearboxes.

What to checkAt viewing, scan the transmission control module for fault code P1740 (18148) and test clutch temperature readings if possible. Ask for maintenance history of the mechatronic and clutch.

Reported oneco-torque.co.uk

DQ200/DQ250 DSG solenoid valve electrical short-circuiting

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

Moisture or oxidation on the mechatronic control unit can cause solenoid electrical failure, leading to erratic shifting or limp mode activation.

What to checkInspect mechatronic unit for corrosion or moisture ingress. Check for erratic shifting, limp mode, or no reverse gear engagement. Use VCDS to verify solenoid signal integrity.

Reported onerenservis.net

DQ250 DSG engine stalling during braking

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The DQ250 DSG gearbox has a known software problem that can cause the engine to stall when braking, especially in stop-and-go traffic. This is due to improper torque management and clutch engagement. It can be a safety concern and may not be found in a standard inspection.

What to checkDuring a test drive, simulate stop-and-go traffic and brake gently to see if the engine stalls. Ask whether the gearbox software has been updated or if any relevant recalls/service bulletins exist.

Reported ontvsengineering.com

DQ250 DSG mechatronic hydraulic pressure failure

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

The DQ250 DSG gearbox has a mechatronic unit that controls hydraulic pressure for gear shifts. This unit can develop chronic pressure regulation problems, causing limp mode and poor gear engagement. Replacing the mechatronic unit is expensive, so a buyer should know this risk is common and predictable on this specific gearbox.

What to checkAt viewing, test drive and feel for jerky or delayed shifts. Check for transmission warning lights and use an OBD scanner to look for stored fault codes related to hydraulic pressure regulation (e.g., pressure or solenoid codes). Also check the DSG fluid condition and service history.

Reported oneco-torque.co.uk

DQ250 DSG needs 60k km oil change despite lifetime oil claim

Serious

The DQ250 gearbox oil is not actually lifetime; the manufacturer recommends changing it every 60,000 km. Using the wrong oil can seriously damage the transmission. If the service history lacks proof of oil changes, assume it is due.

What to checkCheck service records for DQ250 oil changes at 60,000 km intervals; if absent, budget for a change. Verify the correct oil spec is used and look for any gearbox hesitation or jerking during a test drive.

Reported onteknikotomatiksanziman.com

DQ250 DSG premature clutch wear from aggressive shifts

SeriousNoticed on a test drive

The DQ250 is a wet-clutch dual-clutch transmission. Aggressive acceleration and shifting put extra stress on the wet clutches, causing them to wear out prematurely. This can lead to shuddering, slipping, and expensive repairs, making it a critical point for buyers to check.

What to checkDuring test drive, feel for clutch judder or slipping, especially in low gears. Check the DSG service history for fluid and filter changes. Use a diagnostic tool to read clutch wear adaptations and pending gearbox fault codes.

Reported ontvsengineering.com

DQ250 DSG solenoid valves clogged by wear debris

SeriousFound with a diagnostic scan

Inside the gearbox, tiny metal shavings from clutch or gear wear can clog the solenoid valves in the mechatronic unit. This impairs hydraulic control and can force premature replacement of the solenoid valves or the entire mechatronic unit, a costly repair.

What to checkAsk for transmission service history; if fluid is dark or has metallic particles, it indicates wear. A test drive may reveal shift hesitation or jerking. Diagnostic scan may show solenoid valve fault codes.
